A simple Bitcoin strategy anyone can follow without charts, bots or leverage: buy in small fixed amounts when the price falls, sell small slices when it rises, and keep fees close to zero. Below are all 18 trades from February to August 2026, with every number shown.

The playbook in five rules

Each rule comes straight from what the trading log actually did, not from a backtest.

Set a budget, then split it into small tranches

Never go all-in at once. Decide your total budget up front and buy in pieces of roughly 5–15% of it. If the price keeps falling, you still have money left to buy lower.

A $23,000 budget was put in over 15 buys of $1,000–$3,000 each, spread across four months.

Buy more when the price is lower

Make your tranches bigger when Bitcoin drops and smaller when it's high. That pulls your average cost down without any need to time the exact bottom.

Buys of $1,000 near $65–67k. When it fell to $60–63k in June, buys grew to $2,000–$3,000 a day. $14,500 went in during the June dip alone.

Take small profits into strength

When the price is well above your average cost, sell a small slice (a few percent of your stack). You lock in real gains and rebuild cash for the next dip, while keeping most of your Bitcoin.

Four sells, most just 2–7% of holdings, at $65.8k–$81.1k. Together they returned $3,929 in cash and kept 85% of the BTC bought.

Fees are a guaranteed loss, so cut them

A 2–3% fee on every trade wipes out a big part of a short-term gain. Compare platforms and pay attention to the spread, not only the stated fee.

The first $8,500 went through a broker app and cost $160 in fees (~1.9%). The next $14,500 went through an exchange with zero-fee conversion and cost $0.

Write down every trade

Date, amount, price, BTC received, fee. A simple spreadsheet keeps you honest, shows your real average cost, and stops emotion from making your decisions.

The point isn't that the strategy always wins. When prices end very high, all-in wins. When they fall, all-in loses the most. The strategy trades a bit of that upside for less regret, cash in hand, and a lower average cost if the price dips.

Honest lessons

What worked, and what could have gone better.

What worked

  • Buying through the June dip ($58–63k) pulled the average cost down to $63,428.
  • Small sells meant there was never a "sold everything too early" moment.
  • Switching platforms cut trading fees to zero for the rest of the year.

What to improve

  • The July sell at $65,846 was only ~4% above average cost. A clear rule like "sell only at 15% or more above cost" is better.
  • The early broker-app buys lost ~1.9% to fees before the price had moved at all.
  • Buys in March–April at $66–67k were above the final average. Patience pays.
